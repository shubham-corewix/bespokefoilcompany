## 1. Your two asks - both done

**1. Triggers are in the website code.**

`functions/_shared/lifecycle.js` holds every template key and delay in one
place, so a timing change is one edit rather than four functions.

Wired into the three trigger points that live in this repo:

    stripe-webhook          welcome-1/2/3 at +1/+4/+7 days
    submit-lead             welcome-1/2/3 at +0/+3/+6 days, COMMUNITY ONLY
    upload-portal-complete  addons-teaser at +1 day, gated on a ticket

The despatch chain and the in-person welcome are your triggers in n8n. Their
keys are listed in lifecycle.js so both sides use the same names.

Three decisions in there worth knowing about:

- **Transactional never goes through the queue.** A customer who has just paid
  should not wait for a poller. Confirmations stay immediate and direct; only
  scheduled lifecycle mail is queued.
- **Only community sign-ups get the subscriber series.** A franchise enquiry, a
  contact message and a slot reservation all get a human reply - dropping a
  three-part welcome on someone who asked a direct question is how a brand
  starts feeling automated.
- **The add-ons teaser is +1 day, not immediate.** She has just handed over her
  baby's handprints; a sales email in the same minute as the thank-you reads
  badly.

**2. bfc-shell is integrated - for the three CUSTOMER emails.**

Of the ten send sites in this repo, seven are internal notifications: the five
lead alerts, the studio notification, and onboarding. Putting "new contact form
message" through a branded customer shell would make an internal alert look like
a customer email and lose the plain data table that makes it useful.

So these three route through bfc-shell:

    order-confirmation      stripe-webhook.js
    addons-confirmation     addons-stripe-webhook.js
    upload-received         upload-portal-complete.js

The internal seven are unchanged. Shout if you disagree - it is a one-line change
per sender either way.

**Integrated by delegation, not replacement.** Each file's existing send helper
now calls `sendEmail()` first and passes the HTML it already built as
`fallbackHtml`. The old path is not removed, it becomes the safety net: flag
off, `email_content` row missing, template unknown, or Mandrill rejecting all
land on exactly the email customers get today. Verified on all three paths for
both webhooks.

**Still off by default.** `EMAIL_SHELL_ENABLED` and `EMAIL_QUEUE_ENABLED` both
have to be set to `true`. Deploying this changes no email behaviour at all.

**I still do not have your schema**, so the assumed `email_content` /
`email_config` columns and merge variable names are as written in send-email.js
under CONTRACT. That is now safe to leave: the flag is off, and when you switch
it on the log line says `sent via shell` or `sent via inline`, so the first live
send tells you which path actually ran.

**One thing to watch when you do switch it on.** The fallback catches hard
failures - a missing table, an unknown template, a rejected send. It cannot
catch a send that succeeds with the wrong merge variable names, because nothing
errors: Mandrill reports success on a blank email. So the first live send needs
looking at, not just checking for an error.

If your column names differ from my assumption, it is one object in
send-email.js and nothing else.

### Performance

Stripe and `checkout.js` moved out of `<head>` on home, our-kit and
keepsake-standalone. **Moved, not deferred** - `BFCCheckout.init(...)` runs from an
inline block in the body, so a deferred module would load after it and throw.
Placing the tags immediately above the call keeps execution order identical.
Blocking scripts in head went 3 -> 1 on two pages, 2 -> 1 on the third.

Fraunces weight 560 removed site-wide. My first pass only edited the first match
per page and each page has two font URLs (a preload and the stylesheet), so 33
pages ended up with a preload requesting a different URL from the stylesheet -
wasted preload, 560 still downloading. Now identical everywhere.
